# Cultivation MMO — Living Game Vision

> **Status:** Early, provisional vision. Nothing in this document is permanently locked. Any decision may change at any time, and every affected requirement, design choice, and task must change with it.

## 1. North star

Create a third-person, 3D, open-world cultivation life-sandbox MMO where players can rise from ordinary lives to immortality, build lasting powers and civilizations, cross universes, and eventually reach an Omniverse-scale cosmology.

The game should deliver the feeling of cultivating quietly for immense spans of time, emerging with frightening power, founding an eternal home or faction, and discovering that greater heavens still exist. It must not copy an existing story or mobile cultivation game.

The long-term ambition is at least **1,000 players online at the same moment**, with room to grow beyond that. This is a north star, not the target for the first build.

## 2. Current product direction

- **Genre:** Cultivation life sandbox, action RPG, territory builder, and persistent MMO.
- **Camera:** Third-person view behind the character.
- **World:** Open, explorable 3D spaces rather than menu-only travel.
- **Source repository:** Public and verified, so GitHub's standard hosted runners can build at $0. The initial online game world remains private to approved testers.
- **Public-source boundary:** Anyone may read or fork the source and plans, but receives no repository write, game-owner, server, signing, or developer-tool access. Original project rights remain reserved under the repository license.
- **First platform:** iPhone, delivered as an `.ipa` built in GitHub's cloud and privately installed with ESign.
- **Private installation:** The owner uses ESign, not TrollStore or SideStore, and does not need the project designed around a seven-day signing limit.
- **Development pace:** There is no fixed deadline. Correctness, quality, and finishing agreed work matter more than rushing.
- **Current development budget:** **$0 while the owner is the only tester.** No paid plan, billable service, automatic overage, or purchase may be activated without the owner's later explicit approval.
- **Later platforms:** Android and computers, sharing accounts and worlds when practical.
- **Public release:** An official App Store release will eventually be needed to reach a large audience. Private ESign builds are for development and personal testing.
- **Orientation:** Landscape is the working choice for third-person controls.
- **Business model:** Cosmetic and decorative purchases only for now; this may change later. Real money currently must not buy power, fate, Systems, bloodlines, resources, or faster cultivation.

GitHub can build the app, but GitHub does not host the live MMO by itself. Online worlds will also need separately hosted game servers.

## 3. Core design principles

### 3.1 Freedom with consequences

Players are not locked into one role or permanent class. A person may wander, join or found a sect, build a clan or city, trade, craft, teach, rule, hide, fight, explore, reincarnate, or combine several lives and roles.

Freedom does not mean consequence-free play. Relationships, promises, crimes, favors, betrayals, injuries, blood debts, wars, and reputation may follow a character or family for generations.

### 3.2 A harsh and unequal cultivation world

Cultivation is not artificially equal.

- Birth, talent, wealth, family, luck, bloodlines, teachers, opportunities, and Systems may differ greatly.
- Some starts are genuinely better than others.
- Strong starts do not receive fake weaknesses merely to make everyone equal, although believable advantages naturally attract duties, jealousy, enemies, or stronger trials.
- Weak starts may be overcome through effort, cunning, alliances, theft, forbidden methods, rare inheritances, transformation, or reincarnation. Success is possible but never guaranteed.
- A late player may surpass an older player, but the game never promises automatic catch-up.
- As knowledge spreads naturally through the world, early cultivation may become easier over time without erasing veterans' achievements.

Fairness applies to consistent rules, honest odds, anti-cheat, moderation, payments, and essential player protection—not equal power or equal outcomes.

### 3.3 Power must change how play feels

Progression cannot be only larger numbers.

- Mortal combat is grounded, deliberate, and dangerous.
- Cultivation gradually unlocks supernatural speed, air movement, wall movement, ranged force, flight, short-range teleportation, and increasingly large techniques.
- Physical needs also change: mortals need food, rest, warmth, air, and treatment; advanced beings may eventually survive hunger, hostile worlds, space, voids, or bodily destruction.
- Body, sword, spell, soul, technology, divine, psychic, bloodline, and mixed paths should feel different.

### 3.4 A living world

Important NPCs are people, not stationary quest dispensers. They can have goals, memories, relationships, cultivation, jobs, travel, possessions, loyalties, grudges, families, aging, and death. They may create or destroy factions and can become stronger or more influential than players.

Large crowds may use a simpler simulation for performance, while important people receive deeper simulation.

### 3.5 Long lives, legacies, and changing eras

Characters age while living in the world. Breakthroughs extend lifespan. Ordinary logout does not age a character to death. Deliberately entering seclusion can spend in-world time and lifespan in exchange for cultivation progress.

Death does not erase the player's account. Depending on circumstances and advancement, a player may continue through an heir, survive as a soul, rebuild a body, possess a suitable vessel, or reincarnate. Exact death rules remain open.

Players can leave lasting descendants, disciples, techniques, treasures, territories, enemies, and legends.

## 4. Art, tone, and content

The art direction deliberately blends several moods while keeping one identity:

- **Semi-realistic foundation:** believable people, materials, landscapes, and architecture.
- **Bright fantasy:** peaceful lands, prosperous cities, rare treasures, and spectacular techniques.
- **Living ink:** Qi, visions, breakthroughs, menus, transitions, and some powers resemble moving traditional ink art.
- **Dark fantasy:** forbidden regions, demonic paths, ancient ruins, disasters, and tribulations.
- **Higher reality:** advanced realms become stranger, more colorful, and less bound by ordinary geometry.

Violence may be graphic at its highest setting, but gore is cosmetic and adjustable:

- **Off:** no blood or dismemberment; use non-graphic light or ink effects.
- **Low:** mild blood and no dismemberment.
- **Medium:** stronger wounds and limited gore.
- **Full:** complete intended combat effects.
- Separate controls for blood, wounds, dismemberment, and body persistence should be used where practical.

Public stores will rate the game using the strongest content available, even if gore can be disabled.

Romance is optional and only involves adult characters. Marriage, families, heirs, and inherited bloodlines may exist. There will be **no sexual scenes**; private intimacy remains non-explicit or fades to black. Child-stage characters cannot use romance, open PvP, or graphic-gore features.

## 5. World structure

Use a hybrid world:

- Important cities, sects, geography, landmarks, histories, and major story locations are handcrafted.
- Some wilderness details, caves, secret realms, treasures, creatures, and events change over time.
- The world does not wait for one chosen hero. Players and NPCs can change territory, politics, trade, knowledge, and history.

The exact opening story is undecided. The proposed “Age of Awakening” idea—returning spiritual energy and reappearing ancient places—remains only an option.

## 6. Cosmology

The intended scale can grow through:

1. Mortal regions and continents
2. Worlds and higher realms
3. Star systems and great domains
4. Individual universes
5. Multiverses with different histories and laws
6. Realities beyond conventional multiverses
7. The Omniverse, defined by this game as the greatest known collection of realities, timelines, dimensions, laws, and modes of existence

Higher scale must introduce new actions and rules—creation, law-making, space, time, civilization shaping, avatars, and reality changes—not only larger damage values.

Immortals may revisit lower worlds, but strict laws suppress their power or require a limited avatar. This protects lower-level play without pretending the visitor has lost their true advancement.

Different realities may support cultivation, magic, divine power, technology, psychic abilities, martial traditions, bloodlines, or entirely new systems. A person can eventually learn or combine different forms of power, but doing so requires compatibility, knowledge, time, resources, and risk.

## 7. Character beginnings and progression

Players can eventually choose to begin as a child and live through early years, or enter as a young adult with an existing past. Choosing an age does not allow someone to select the objectively strongest destiny from a menu.

Exact talent, body, ancestry, local conditions, family power, opportunities, and hidden fate are influenced by the living world. Deleting characters or making accounts repeatedly must not become a reliable way to farm rare origins; the exact protection will be designed later.

There are no permanent classes. A character may learn any path they can access and survive, but mastery is limited by time, resources, teachers, understanding, bodily compatibility, conflicting energies, and danger. Starting talent does not have to be a permanent ceiling: roots, bodies, bloodlines, souls, and methods may be repaired, stolen, evolved, replaced, or rebuilt when the world provides a believable way.

## 8. Systems and transmigration

Distinguish ordinary game UI from a true in-world System:

- Every player needs normal interface elements such as health, inventory, controls, and settings. These do not automatically count as a supernatural System in the fiction.
- True personal Systems are rare powers. Most native people never receive one.
- Natives may awaken, inherit, discover, steal, merge, or eventually create Systems.
- Transmigration is itself extremely rare, but the working idea is that roughly nine out of ten transmigrators arrive with or attract some form of System. The number is provisional.

A System may focus on seclusion, domains, sects, cities, finding opportunities, analyzing techniques, tasks, crafting, survival, copying, or devouring. Systems are unequal, but none should function as an unexplained button for immediate, infinite power.

A System can have a tier, specialty, source, cost, rules, limited knowledge, and growth path. Depending on its nature, it may be incomplete, sleeping, deceptive, damaged, sealed, stolen, resisted, or capable of betraying its host. Extremely advanced beings may create Systems for followers or worlds.

## 9. Seclusion

Seclusion is a real style of play rather than a passive timer alone.

Before a retreat, a player chooses a location, method, resources, protection, and intended duration. Progress depends on spiritual conditions, formations, teachers, comprehension, supplies, interruptions, and danger. A retreat may succeed, stall, reveal insight, create injury, or cause an important event.

Limited progress can continue while the player is away. Major breakthroughs and major danger require the player to return and participate. This supports long cultivation times without turning the entire game into autoplay.

## 10. Combat and PvP

Combat is real-time and directly controlled.

The overall style is adaptive:

- Early play is closer to heavy, grounded combat where timing, positioning, blocking, and dodging matter.
- Higher advancement allows faster movement, combinations, flight, ranged techniques, and spectacular powers.
- A body path may remain weighty while sword or movement paths become extremely fast.

PvP uses several levels of danger:

- **Safe regions:** no unwanted PvP.
- **Duels:** all participants explicitly agree; no item loss.
- **Dangerous regions:** open PvP, rare resources, and greater rewards.
- **Beginner protection and realm suppression:** powerful characters cannot freely prey on beginners or ignore lower-world laws.

The current dangerous-region loss rule is balanced: a defeated player drops materials gathered in that expedition but keeps equipped gear, learned techniques, and permanent progress. Blood debts, bounties, faction responses, and revenge may continue after the fight.

## 11. Territory, civilization, and creation

Territory exists physically in the 3D world rather than only in menus.

A player may begin with a camp, home, cave, or pocket space and grow it into an estate, clan, sect, city, kingdom, civilization, world, or greater creation. Small projects allow direct placement. Larger powers require plans, resources, workers, knowledge, logistics, and delegation to trusted players or NPCs.

Residents may serve as disciples, relatives, workers, craftspeople, guards, officials, teachers, or leaders. Territory can provide safety and strong defensive advantages, but it still follows the rules of its region and the wider world.

At the highest scale, creation may include planets, universes, local laws, or Systems.

## 12. Social life and families

Players may build relationships with NPCs and, with mutual consent, other players. Features may include friendship, mentorship, discipleship, rivalry, sworn bonds, adult romance, marriage, adoption, descendants, clans, inheritance, and succession.

Children can inherit appearance, bloodlines, talents, property, obligations, enemies, and family reputation. They are never involved in sexual content. Player communication and relationship tools will require blocking, reporting, privacy, and age-appropriate safeguards before public release.

## 13. MMO operation and money

The world must treat the server—not a player's phone—as the authority for movement limits, combat outcomes, inventory, trades, cultivation rewards, and important random results. This is necessary for anti-cheat and a trustworthy unequal world.

Growth should occur in stages:

0. A zero-cost solo, owner-targeted technical preview, using local/on-device simulation and free standard GitHub-hosted runners in the public source repository
1. One developer/owner in a private online build after a genuinely zero-cost option is verified or the owner later approves a budget
2. A few invited testers
3. Small shared-world tests
4. Regional and persistence load tests
5. Public testing
6. Gradual scale toward 1,000+ simultaneous players

The $0 stage does not cancel the online MMO design. It prevents accidental spending while only the owner is testing. If a safe service cannot guarantee zero cost, its deployment remains prepared but inactive until the owner chooses a budget; security, privacy, backups, and owner-only access must never be weakened merely to use a free service.

**Railway Hobby is the current preferred candidate for the first paid online test.** It may become the permanent provider if real testing goes well, but neither purchase nor permanent use is decided. Its current advertised $5 figure is a minimum commitment with usage-based charges possible above the included credit, so activation requires a newly approved monthly maximum and the checklist in `docs/COST_DECISIONS.md`.

For now, money may purchase only cosmetic presentation and decorations. Paid items should not be exchangeable for game currency or power. The first playable version contains no purchases.

### 13.1 Owner-only developer and operations tools

The detailed, current control and security plan is maintained in `docs/OWNER_OPERATIONS_SECURITY.md`.

The owner needs complete, phone-friendly tools for every game and service feature that is implemented. A feature is not operationally complete until the owner can inspect it, diagnose problems, safely change or repair relevant state, and understand what happened.

The current tool direction includes:

- Build versions, release controls, server health, performance, logs, alerts, costs, deploys, rollbacks, backups, and restoration
- Test-account invites, session revocation, player moderation, announcements, and emergency world controls
- Character, inventory, equipment, cultivation, ability, NPC, creature, resource, quest/event, duel, territory, weather, time, and world-state inspection
- Safe owner commands to teleport, spawn or remove test content, adjust or repair state, start or stop events, reset broken state, and change feature flags
- A separate test environment so dangerous experiments do not default to the live world

These controls must use a separate owner console designed for an iPhone browser. Regular player builds must not contain an unlockable developer menu. Hiding a button is not security: every privileged command must be approved by the server for the single configured owner identity using strong authentication and fresh confirmation for dangerous actions. No player, tester, moderator, or future helper receives developer access unless the owner explicitly changes this rule.

Privileged actions must be recorded in an append-only audit trail. Destructive actions require a clear warning and, where practical, a recoverable snapshot. The owner must be able to revoke all privileged sessions immediately. No owner credential or master key may be embedded in an `.ipa`, ordinary website code, logs, or the repository.

No distributable `.ipa`, including the owner's public-prerelease test copy, contains a developer menu or local diagnostic overlay. Runtime diagnostics are collected safely by normal game code and viewed only through the authenticated owner console. Copying or modifying an `.ipa` must reveal no privileged tool and must provide no privileged server access.

## 14. First playable version

Before the complete online first playable, the project may produce a **zero-cost solo, owner-targeted technical preview** that runs the same game rules locally/on-device to prove the `.ipa`, movement, camera, and early gameplay without a paid server. Its unsigned package is publicly downloadable from a clearly labeled prerelease, but it remains a solo preview—not proof that multiplayer or online persistence is finished.

The complete first playable `.ipa` remains a small online foundation, not a miniature Omniverse. Its working scope is:

- One private online valley
- A village, forest, mountains, and cave
- Character appearance creation using a young-adult character for the prototype
- Third-person touch movement and camera
- Basic grounded sword and unarmed combat
- Gathering herbs and finding items
- Meditation, cultivation progress, and one meaningful breakthrough
- A few named NPCs and creatures
- A small home or cultivation cave to claim and decorate
- Persistent online character saving
- A few players able to share the region and use agreed duels
- All gore levels, clear mobile settings, and no monetization
- A separate, strongly authenticated owner console with controls for every implemented feature; tester builds contain no developer menu
- A GitHub cloud build that produces an `.ipa` suitable for ESign

Later versions add full childhood, aging, reincarnation, true Systems, deep families, dangerous-region PvP, advanced building, larger worlds, major factions, economies, and cosmic progression.

## 15. Open decisions

These are intentionally unresolved:

- Final game title and world names
- Opening-era story and central history
- Exact character-origin selection and anti-reroll rules
- Exact death, soul survival, inheritance, and reincarnation rules
- Realm names and full progression ladder
- Playable species and transformation rules
- Time scale between shared worlds, seclusion, and higher realms
- Exact kinds, origins, and frequency of Systems
- Economy, crafting depth, trade, and territory war rules
- Guild, clan, sect, chat, voice, moderation, and community tools
- Exact engine, network transport, server host, and release infrastructure
- Store products, prices, and whether the cosmetic-only business model ever changes

Every open decision should be tested against the north star, technical reality, player safety, long-term server costs, and the user's latest direction.
